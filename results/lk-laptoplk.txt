            How to Back Up Your Files: Cloud, External Drive or Both?

Oct 26, 2026

              Introduction

Every laptop and hard drive will fail one day. It might be a dead SSD, a spilled drink, a stolen bag, a virus, or a power cut at the wrong moment. If your only copy of your assignments, work files or family photos is on one device, you are one accident away from losing them.
Backing up is simple once you know how. This guide explains your options, the pros and cons of each, and an easy method that protects almost everyone.
Why Backups Matter

Hardware failure: Drives wear out and can fail without warning.
Accidents: Water, drops and power surges damage laptops every day.
Theft or loss: If your laptop is gone, so are your files unless they are stored elsewhere.
Viruses and ransomware: Some malware locks or deletes your files.
Human error: Deleting or overwriting the wrong file happens to everyone.
Option 1: Cloud Backup

Cloud backup stores copies of your files on internet servers, using services such as Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud.
Advantages

Safe from local disasters: Your files survive theft, fire or flooding at home.
Access anywhere: Open your files from any device.
Automatic: Once set up, files sync without effort.
Disadvantages

Needs internet: Uploading large folders, such as videos and photos, can take a long time on slower connections.
Storage limits: Free plans are small, and larger storage usually needs a paid plan.
Sync is not always a true backup: If you delete a file on your laptop, a sync service may delete it from the cloud too. Check how your service handles deleted files and version history.
Option 2: External Drive Backup

An external hard drive or portable SSD connects to your laptop by USB and stores a copy of your files locally.
Advantages

Fast: Copying large files is quick, with no internet required.
One time cost: No monthly fee.
Large capacity: Easily hold videos, photos and full project archives.
Disadvantages

Can be lost or damaged: If the drive sits next to your laptop, a theft, fire or power surge can destroy both.
Needs you to remember: Unless you automate it, backups only happen when you plug in the drive.
Drives fail too: No drive lasts forever, so do not rely on a single copy.
External hard drive or portable SSD?

External hard drive (HDD): More storage for the money, good for large archives. It has moving parts, so handle it gently.
Portable SSD: Faster, quieter and more resistant to knocks, a good choice if you carry it around.
The Best Answer: Use Both

The most reliable approach is a combination, often called the 3-2-1 rule.
3 copies: Your working files, plus two backups.
2 different types of storage: For example your laptop and an external drive.
1 copy somewhere else: For example in the cloud, away from your home or office.
In practice, this means keeping your files on your laptop, backing them up regularly to an external drive, and keeping your most important files in the cloud as well. If one fails, you still have the others.
Which Method Suits You?

Students: Cloud storage for assignments and projects, plus a flash drive or external drive for a second copy before exams.
Office professionals: Cloud sync for daily work, with a regular external backup of important folders.
Photographers and video editors: Large files need an external HDD or portable SSD, with a cloud copy of final deliverables.
Families: Keep photos in the cloud and on an external drive, and check both once a month.
A Simple Backup Routine

Decide what matters: Documents, photos, projects and anything you cannot replace.
Set up cloud sync: Turn on automatic sync for your key folders.
Back up to an external drive: Do it weekly, or daily if you work on important projects.
Test your backup: Once in a while, open a few files from the backup to confirm they work.
Keep one copy away from your laptop: Do not store the drive in the same bag as the laptop.
Protect Your Backups From Power Problems

Sudden power cuts and surges can damage drives and corrupt files while they are being written. Use a UPS or a good surge protector for desktops, and avoid unplugging drives while files are copying. Always use the safe eject option.

You do not need to be technical to protect your files. Set up automatic cloud sync, add an external drive for a second copy, and check them from time to time. It takes less than an hour to set up and can save you from losing years of work.

            How to Choose a Laptop for Video Editing and Design in Sri Lanka

Oct 26, 2026

              Introduction

Video editing, photo editing and graphic design push a laptop much harder than office work. Timelines stutter, exports take forever and apps crash when the hardware is not up to the job. Choosing the wrong laptop costs you time every single day.
This guide explains which parts matter most for creative work, how much of each you really need, and which laptops in our current range fit different types of creators.
What Matters Most for Creative Work
Processor (CPU)

The processor does much of the heavy lifting when you edit, render and export. For video editing and design, aim for at least an AMD Ryzen 7, Intel Core i7 or Intel Core 7 class chip. A Ryzen 5 or Core i5 can handle photo editing and light video work, but exports will be slower.
RAM (Memory)

RAM is where your editing software keeps footage, layers and previews while you work. 16GB is the realistic minimum for creative work today. Light photo editing can get by on 8GB, but video, large design files and multitasking all run far better on 16GB, and heavy 4K projects benefit from 32GB.
Check whether the RAM is upgradeable before you buy. Many laptops have soldered memory that cannot be expanded later.
Graphics (GPU)

A dedicated graphics card, such as an NVIDIA RTX, speeds up many tasks in editing and design software, including effects, previews, colour work and exports. Laptops with only integrated graphics can still do creative work, but a dedicated GPU makes video editing noticeably smoother. Check that your editing software supports GPU acceleration.
Storage

Footage and design files are large. Use a fast NVMe SSD of at least 512GB for your system and active projects, and 1TB if you can. Plan to keep finished projects on external storage so your laptop does not fill up.
Display

For design and colour sensitive work, look for a Full HD or WUXGA IPS screen at the very least. A taller 16:10 screen gives more room for timelines and toolbars. If colour accuracy is critical to your work, check the display specifications carefully, because many laptop screens do not cover the full colour range that professional work needs.
Cooling and build

Editing keeps the processor and graphics busy for long periods, and Sri Lanka's warm climate makes cooling even more important. Laptops with stronger cooling hold their speed longer instead of slowing down when hot.
How Much Power Do You Need?

Photo editing and basic design: Core i5 or Ryzen 5, 16GB RAM, SSD storage. A dedicated GPU is helpful but not essential.
Regular video editing (1080p): Ryzen 7 or Core i7 class chip, 16GB RAM, dedicated RTX graphics, 512GB to 1TB NVMe SSD.
Heavy editing, 4K and motion graphics: The strongest processor and graphics you can get, 32GB RAM if possible, and 1TB or more of fast storage.

            Why RAM Prices Are Rising: How the AI Boom Is Affecting Your Next Laptop or PC

Oct 17, 2026

              Introduction

If you have looked at laptops, desktops or memory upgrades recently, you may have noticed that RAM has become more expensive. It is not your imagination, and it is not limited to Sri Lanka. Memory prices have been climbing around the world.
The main reason is the artificial intelligence (AI) boom. This guide explains what is happening in simple terms, how it affects people who buy laptops and PCs, and what you can do to avoid overpaying.
What Is RAM and Why Does It Matter?

RAM (Random Access Memory) is your computer's short term working memory. It holds the apps, browser tabs and files you are using right now so the processor can reach them instantly. More RAM means your laptop can handle more things at once without slowing down.
Every laptop, desktop, phone and server depends on memory chips, which is why a change in memory supply affects almost everything we buy.
Why RAM Prices Are Rising
AI needs enormous amounts of memory

AI data centres run thousands of powerful processors, and each one needs very fast memory to feed it data. The kind of memory used for this is called HBM (High Bandwidth Memory), along with large amounts of high capacity server memory.
Manufacturers are following the profit

Only a few companies in the world make memory chips. HBM and server memory sell at much higher margins than the everyday DDR4 and DDR5 memory used in consumer laptops and PCs, so manufacturers have been shifting production capacity toward them.
Consumer memory supply gets squeezed

The same factories and materials are used for all of these chips. When more capacity goes to AI memory, less is left for consumer DDR4 and DDR5. When demand stays strong and supply tightens, prices go up. Industry reports also suggest manufacturers are scaling down older DDR4 production, so even older memory is not automatically cheaper.
Note: memory markets change quickly. Treat this as the general picture and check current availability when you buy.
How This Affects Buyers in Sri Lanka

Laptop and desktop prices: Memory is one of the main components in any computer, so higher RAM costs can push up the price of complete systems, especially models with 16GB or more.
RAM upgrades and kits: Buying separate RAM for an upgrade can cost more than it did a year or two ago.
Older DDR4 is not a guaranteed bargain: As production moves on, DDR4 supply can tighten too.
Fake and low quality memory risk: When genuine RAM is expensive, cheap unbranded modules look tempting. These often cause crashes and have no real warranty.
Should You Buy Now or Wait?

Nobody can predict memory prices with certainty. A sensible way to decide is to look at your own situation.
Buy now if: Your current laptop is slow and holding back your work or studies, or you already found a model that suits you. Waiting has its own cost if you cannot work properly.
Consider waiting if: Your current setup works fine and you were only planning a nice to have upgrade.
Either way, avoid panic buying a machine that is more than you need.
Smart Ways to Handle Higher RAM Costs
Buy the right amount, not the biggest amount

8GB: Fine for basic browsing, documents and online classes.
16GB: The comfortable choice for office work, multitasking, students and light creative work.
32GB and above: Only needed for heavy video editing, 3D work, virtual machines or serious development.
Check whether your laptop's RAM can be upgraded

Many modern laptops have RAM soldered to the motherboard, so it can never be upgraded. Others use a removable SO-DIMM module. If your laptop has soldered memory, it is better to buy the right amount on day one. Always check your exact model, or ask our team, before buying parts.
Fix slowdowns for free first

Before spending on RAM, close unused start up apps, free up storage and check Task Manager. If memory usage is not near its limit, more RAM will not help.
Match the RAM type to your device

DDR4 and DDR5 are not interchangeable. Laptops use SO-DIMM modules and desktops use full size DIMMs. Always check your exact model before buying.
Stick with genuine, branded memory

Buy from a seller who can stand behind the product with a warranty. Poor quality RAM can cost you far more in lost work than you saved on the price.

For most people, 16GB is the comfortable standard. This guide explains why, who needs it, and which 16GB laptops in our current range suit different needs.
8GB vs 16GB: What Actually Changes?

8GB: Works for browsing, documents and online classes, but can slow down when you open many tabs, run video calls and use office apps together.
16GB: Handles heavy multitasking smoothly, keeps the laptop usable for more years, and gives you room as software grows.
If you only do light tasks, 8GB can still do the job. If you multitask, study, work from home or want a laptop to last, 16GB is the safer choice.
Who Really Needs 16GB?

Office professionals: Email, spreadsheets, browser tabs and video meetings all running together.
University students: Research, presentations, online lectures and assignments open at once.

            AI PCs and Copilot+ Explained: Do You Actually Need One?

Oct 10, 2026

              Introduction

Open any laptop ad today and you will see the same phrase: "AI PC". Some also say "Copilot+". It sounds important, but for most buyers it raises more questions than it answers. Is it just a marketing label? Will your laptop be slower without it? Is it worth paying more for?
This guide explains what an AI PC really is, what the features do in everyday life, and who should care. By the end, you will know whether it belongs on your shopping list or whether you can safely ignore it.
What Is an AI PC?

An AI PC is a laptop with a dedicated chip for artificial intelligence tasks, called an NPU (Neural Processing Unit). It sits alongside the two chips you already know about.
The CPU (processor): Handles general tasks such as opening apps, running spreadsheets and browsing.
The GPU (graphics): Handles visuals such as video, games and design work.
The NPU: Handles repetitive AI workloads, such as blurring your video call background or generating live captions, quickly and with very little battery drain.
The point of the NPU is efficiency. AI tasks can run on the CPU or GPU too, but they use more power and slow down other things you are doing. An NPU takes that load off them.
What Does Copilot+ Mean?

Copilot+ PC is a label created by Microsoft for Windows laptops that meet a higher standard. The key requirement is an NPU capable of 40 or more TOPS (trillions of operations per second), together with at least 16GB of RAM and 256GB of storage. Laptops that qualify unlock an extra set of Windows AI features.
This is where buyers get confused. Many newer laptops include an NPU and are described as "AI ready", but not all of them reach the 40 TOPS bar. In short: every Copilot+ PC is an AI PC, but not every AI PC is a Copilot+ PC. Always check the exact model before assuming.
What Do AI Features Do in Everyday Use?

The features differ by laptop and by Windows version, and Microsoft keeps adding more. Today the most useful ones are practical rather than flashy.
Better video calls: Background blur, automatic framing and eye contact correction that run without slowing your laptop.
Live captions and translation: Real time subtitles for meetings, videos and calls, useful for students and anyone working across languages.
Smarter creative tools: Faster photo clean up, background removal and image generation inside supported apps.
Better battery life on AI tasks: Because the NPU is built for these jobs, they use far less power than on a regular chip.
More private processing: Some tasks run on the laptop itself instead of being sent to the cloud.
AI PC vs a Regular Laptop: What Actually Changes?

For everyday work such as email, documents, browsing and streaming, a regular laptop with a good processor and enough RAM performs the same. An AI PC does not make Word or Chrome faster. The difference shows up only when you use AI features, and even then mainly in battery life and smoothness.
That means the sensible question is not "AI or not?" but "will I use AI features often enough to care?" If the answer is yes, an NPU is a real benefit. If the answer is no, you would be paying for a feature that sits idle.
Who Benefits Most from an AI PC

Professionals on frequent video calls: Cleaner calls and live captions help every day.
Students: Live captions, translation and writing support tools are useful for lectures and research.
Content creators: Faster AI assisted editing, and less drain on the battery while working away from a power point.
Buyers who want to stay current: If you keep a laptop for four to five years, AI features are likely to become more common in Windows and everyday apps over that time.
Who Can Skip It

If you mainly browse, type documents, watch videos and use online classes, a regular laptop will serve you just as well, and you can put the money toward more RAM or faster storage, which you will notice every day. The same applies to a tight budget. A reliable 16GB machine with an SSD often gives you more real world value than an AI label.
What to Check Before You Buy

NPU performance: Look for the TOPS rating. For the Copilot+ label, it needs to be 40 or higher.
RAM: 16GB is the sensible target for AI features and for general multitasking.
Storage: An NVMe SSD of 512GB or more leaves room for apps and files.
Battery and build: AI features are most useful on the move, so battery life matters.
Official warranty: A newer technology is a good reason to buy from a seller who can support it locally.
